# Starting writing to stop despairing

There's a lot of screaming in my head.

You see, I'm not fully satisfied with work. I normally say that it's fine, or that I'm "content" with it. That may be so - I don't have the clarity of mind to judge, not right now. But something that I dedicate 8 hours of time, basically 1/3rd of a day or 50% of the time I spend being concious, I'd like to be really good. I want to say: I love what I'm doing, it's a shame there isn't more time in the day so that I could do it more.

Instead, I am fucking drained. It's not like I work too hard. But you know how it goes. You are just trying to relax, and the little gremlins inside your head are constantly whispering: "work, bro, remember work, bro, it's really important, bro". Apparently, spending 8 hours a day on something affects your subconscious and makes you think about it - who could've known!

Every day I go to the daily meeting and report on my progress. Actually, I make it up - I usually don't have much progress to share, so I make up some things that I could *plausibly* do to appear like I'm not crashing out really badly. At least, I don't have consistent progress that the managers like - I work at irregular patterns. So I make something up.

This is usually when I have coffee - though doing coffee is a massive struggle, so I usually wait for my partner to brew it for me, so that installs an external dependency to my fucked timetable. And this isn't a robust dependency - apparently people can be tired or sleep or feel unwell. And I can't function until I've had coffee. Well, not that coffee helps much, but I like to have something to latch on to, for stability.

Really, I should be able to brew coffee myself.  It's not that hard. But I can't. I am just so fucking drained.

So then, I reply to coworkers messaging me. Take a look at the Jira board. Sulk. Sometimes I even do something, but that really depends. Either way, even if I'm not actively doing something, or even planning to do anything for the day - I have to be alert. Check new messages. Reply to people. Come up with white lies on what the progress is.

There are two things that really get me.

## Corporate setting

Need I say more?

There have been better pieces on it than I can muster. You know the drill. But it's my blog, my rules, so you're gonna listen to my pain anyway.

I can't fucking work when I'm being pestered and nagged by everybody on when X or Y is going to be done. What the fuck is your problem, anyway? I have the daily meetings where I report my "progress". But of course, that's not enough - why would it be? We have deadlines, goals, quarters, expectations. Work needs to be done! So I can't **actually** say what I haven't been working on - I need to come up with white lies to show that those goals are being reached, that we're going somewhere.

But there are other people in this. They also have tickets. They need to come up with white lies, too. Report on progress. Go to meetings. And they depend on me to do something that's blocking them - so they reach out to me and ask. And I give them estimates and progress to relay to their managers. And this happens in reverse, too - sometimes I need someone to do something before I can proceed, so I have to nag them back. This is a fucking free-for-all, we're brawling each other to death until someone gets unblocked, because murdering a colleague was on their ~~bingo~~ Jira card.

But I'm also getting nagged by my own manager - have I been able to make progress? I'm being quiet during the day. There's no progress reported in Jira. And let me ask you this: *why in the fuck would I report progress in Jira?* I have a daily meeting that I can't skip out on to report progress. My colleagues don't look at Jira, they just message me. And the same true for me too. Because Jira is a fucking piece of shit that is inconvenient to work with. And then there are ticket comments - which are spammed by our VCS integration with every fucking commit, so no one ever reads the comments due to the noise, and they don't check the VCS either, because those random fucking commits don't mean anything. So they ask.

This isn't the worst I experienced, by far, but still - so much micromanagement and communication taxation really gets to me, all too often.

So of course, with so much energy down the drain on communication, there's not much left on doing the actual work. And actual work is not pretty, either. Like, there's a lot of tech debt - and there's neither time nor energy to clean it up. We gotta pump those tickets. So the ugly hacks and temporary workarounds and corner-cutting gets solidified into years-long problems. But hey, at least we get epics on fixing *something* every quarter. So at least some work gets done there.

The thing is, why? There is no greater cause to this. This corporate inefficiency doesn't serve a higher purpose - it's just management and reports that no one reads and shareholder value. I'm not motivated to put in the effort or endure this torture. No one is. It's just a job, silly.

## General tech bullshittery

I'm so fucking tired of tech, too.

Even at its best, tech work isn't pretty. It's amazingly fun to fiddle with stuff, learn more about the problem domain, work with cool people on something you all care about. But you have dependencies. Your build system doesn't support feature X. It can't support it, because this feature X depends on the compiler supporting feature Y. The technical mumbo-jumbo isn't the point - the point is that you need to rely on somebody, who relies on somebody, who relies on somebody.

And this is natural. You can't go and fix it yourself - there's extreme expertise required to do that. People are doing their best and trying to get the features you so want in. But it takes time. In corporate world, you nag someone to death or say "I can't do it". But more generally, what you do is write down the notes, and hold off on doing your thing - or implement a workaround. The happy path is that the workaround isn't the worst thing you've done in your career, and you can do the proper thing later.

As an engineer, you get taught early on that engineering is full of compromises and imperfections. This is true, and it sucks. But software development is also an art, and a craft. A good artist needs to express themselves, and a good craftsman cares about quality. And let me tell you, our artists and craftsmen aren't in a good position.

There are people who just do their small projects all the time, without looking at other people and existing implementations, just for fun. I admire them. But I'm not one of those people. I need a purpose and a reason behind the projects I'm doing. Sometimes, it can be learning this or that or simply fun - Advent of Code is an example of this. But more often, I want to work on things people use, things people develop. And it's a struggle. Even when I'm overall more motivated - I am so exhausted and powerless after the day job that I don't have any tolerance for factors outside of my control. You often need to wait for someone to push a fix, or work around an issue caused by someone having done a poor job, for one reason or another - and I'm doing this all day long but in the worst way, so doing this as a hobby too? Nah.

Software field has been my passion for years, but my passion is getting dried up. I don't think it's permanent - I think I need to take a break. Not only from work, mind you - though that comes with a whole range of practical and annoying considerations, but also from doing it as a hobby. Because the reality is, if a hobby is deriving me only pain and frustration instead of enjoyment and excitement - I am just putting myself in harm's way by continuing this.

I'm not abandoning everything - just getting my priorities straight, and my intentions gay. I won't fiddle with shit I don't find immediately required or fulfilling anymore. There are some projects I'm contributing to, and I'll continue to do so at my pace - because they are fun and I care. But gone are the days when I idly scrolled through the list of random services one can run on a Linux machine and decided on a whim that I really need to deploy that one, right now. I think they'll return - but I won't force it anymore.

## Reclaiming some agency

I still need a hobby. So I'll do writing, starting now.

I have a gigantic stash of ideas in need of being written down. I have held off for years on writing some of them. I've been talking to people throughout, and expressing those ideas, and sometimes making mental notes on where to find it and sometimes bookmarking them in my browser, but there's no consistency with it, and I end up repeating the same things over and over for no real reason. Writing is an excellent way to share knowledge, as it requires understanding the subject, being able to explain it concisely, and delivering it in a way that is hopelessly unfunny. Writing can also be used to share experiences, which I'm currently doing, so off to a great start, I guess?

I will write fiction too, later. I have experience with it. Don't ask me when I've done it last, though.

It's been a long time coming. A really long time. Really, unforgivably long time. It's been 2 years of yearning (get it?) and 2 years of feeling like I suck and my SSG sucks even more so I can do the same thing from scratch and learn HTML+CSS+JS in the meantime, pinky promise. Well, famous last words, as they say.

And since it's been a long time, I have a lot to say. About tech industry, about programming languages, about communities and how they rise and fall, about so many other grievances and other shit that can be interesting or really fucking boring, I can't tell. But I'd like to tell my tales nonetheless.

This might as well be a call to action, of sorts. If you find yourself in a similar position to mine - go ahead and start writing. It's fun and therapeutic. Don't go in circles thinking about building a perfect website - just start somewhere, your thoughts are what matters. Just please, do an authentic thing. And don't choose Medium or Substack - they are really horrible platforms with human-hating policies, and the pennies they offer for posting aren't worth the humiliation.

So yeah, guess I'm in my blogger phase now. Subscribe and smash that motherfucking like button for some hot takes. And if you are hot and want to be smashed, I take $99.99 to sell my influencer integrity, so call me, I dare you.
